Say

ecouter.js is a featherlight, instrumented text-to-speech library in one file, no dependencies. Type a sentence; a formant throat says it in the house voice, and every stage of the sound is yours to measure, draw and change.

What is inside: a Klatt cascade of five formants with life, a second vent for hisses and bursts, a nasal zero, a ceiling at the lips and a governor; a spectrogram gust and its sound; a chain of settings; the 41 phoneme codes of NRL Report 7948; the Report's 355 letter-to-sound rules, public domain since 1985, and their matcher.

This page tests all of it at once: the rules read the text, the throat sings it, the chain tunes it, and the wave and the picture below are of what you hear. The toggles are the tunings laid on the rough signal; flip one and the text is said again.

Two graphs: the waveform, then the spectrogram.
0 to 4 kHz, 60 dB deep. The voice displays in stripes.

Options:
Gust: The spectrogram blurred a little and sound-matched.
Silk Stocking: A shelf down above 4 kHz.
Entone: A comb on the score's own pitch.
Ess: A standard 'de-esser'.
Governor: Holds what stands above 5.5 kHz within hearing.
Mellow: A kind microphone. Highs softened, a small lift below 250 Hz, and the echo of a small room.
Conduction: The tune. Each sentence begins high, sags a little, and returns to level. Without it the voice falls a fifth across the whole, and sounds defeated. Conduction lifts the performance to the ears.
4x FGL: The fast search of Perraudin (2013). Ten turns with momentum replace forty. Judge by ear.

Each sentence is made in its own Web Worker, in parallel. A sentence plays when it and the one before it are done. Where workers cannot start, sentences are made one by one.
